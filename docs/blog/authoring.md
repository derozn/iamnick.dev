# Writing a Post, start to finish

> The author's guide. This is for Nick, not for the build. It covers the whole journey: idea,
> draft, preview, pipeline, publish. The system-side detail lives in
> `.claude/skills/publish-post/SKILL.md` and `docs/blog/blog-built.md` §6.

The one rule over everything: **you draft, AI edits, never ghost-writes.** The passes fix facts,
strip AI tells and catch banned terms. Depth, opinion and voice come from your draft. A thin draft
in means a competently edited thin draft out.

## 1. Have the thing to say

The blog is "Nick's rambles". Anything goes. For build-journal posts the raw material already
exists, generated by the delivery process for exactly this purpose:

- `docs/blog/journal.md` — dated paragraphs, one per build stage, surprises included.
- `docs/blog/discovery.md` — the numbered decision log.
- `docs/blog/agentic-workflow.md` — every rejected idea, with reasons.
- `docs/redesign/blog-handoff.md` §7 — the gotchas.

## 2. Draft it

Your words, rough is fine. Chat spellings get tidied later; rhythm survives. Three ways that all
stay on the right side of the ghost-writing line:

1. **Write it straight into the file.** The obvious way.
2. **Interview mode.** Ask Claude to interview you about the topic. Your chat answers become the
   draft, assembled in your words. This is how `voice.md` was calibrated, and it is the fastest
   route to a real post when you know the story but haven't written it.
3. **Fact sheet first.** Ask Claude to pull the relevant journal entries, decisions and commits
   into a fact sheet, then write from it.

What Claude will not do is write the prose from nothing. If you ask, expect a refusal and this
paragraph quoted back at you.

## 3. Create the file

`content/blog/<slug>.mdx`. The slug is the filename and the URL. Frontmatter contract (Velite
fails the build on violations):

```yaml
---
title: Between 3 and 99 characters
description: Between 10 and 160 characters. This is the meta description, write it as one.
date: 2026-07-24
tags: ['agentic-workflows']
draft: true
slug: must-match-the-filename
---
```

Current Tag vocabulary: `agentic-workflows`, `meta`, `process`. The vocabulary stays deliberately
small; a new Tag is a deliberate choice the pipeline will ask you about, not a free-for-all.
Body starts at h2 if you use headings at all (the template owns the h1). Code blocks get
Shiki-highlighted at build time for free.

## 4. Preview it

`pnpm dev`, then open `/blog/<slug>`. You get the real reading template with a red Draft banner.
The Draft is invisible everywhere else: not on the index, not in tags, sitemap or RSS, and its
URL 404s in production. Preview is a local affordance only; there is no staging surface.

## 5. Run the pipeline

When the draft says what you want it to say:

```
/publish-post <slug>
```

Four passes run, mechanical before judgmental. Structural (frontmatter, slug, tags, build). Fact
and SEO (the post-reviewer agent checks every claim against the repo; the posts are about this
repo, so the repo is the fact source). Voice (avoid-ai-writing edits toward `voice.md`, touching
only what reads as AI). Glossary (banned `_Avoid_` terms). Any pass can be skipped with
`--skip=<pass>`.

**Then it stops.** Gate 1 is you reading the edited draft against a changelog of what every pass
changed and why. Edit whatever you like; your line-edits are also the calibration input for
`voice.md`. Nothing proceeds without your explicit go-ahead.

## 6. Ship and publish

On your approval the pipeline flips `draft: false`, branches `post/<slug>`, runs `pnpm validate`,
gives the diff a low-effort code review (broken MDX, not prose), and opens the PR with the
changelog in the body.

Gate 2 is the merge. **Publishing is you merging the PR.** Nothing auto-merges, ever. On merge the
Post goes live everywhere at once from the one `publishedPosts` read: the index (it takes the next
fig. number), its own page and OG card, the sitemap, RSS, and any tag pages it belongs to.

## 7. Afterwards

The pipeline offers to append a dated paragraph to `docs/blog/journal.md` if the run surfaced
anything worth keeping. After three published Posts the pipeline itself gets reviewed: any pass
that never changed an outcome is deleted, and the pruning becomes a post.
